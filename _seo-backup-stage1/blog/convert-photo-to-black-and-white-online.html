What You Should Know Before Using This Tool

Black and white photo conversion sounds simple — and honestly, it is. But before you click that first button, there are a few things worth understanding so you don't end up disappointed with the result.

First, grayscale and black-and-white are slightly different. Grayscale uses 256 shades of gray, giving you a smooth, continuous tonal range. True "black and white" (or bitmap) only has pure black and pure white pixels — like a newspaper print effect. For most people, grayscale is what they're actually looking for, and it's what nearly every online tool provides by default.

Second, the quality of your original image matters. A blurry or low-resolution color photo will still be blurry and low-resolution in black and white. Converting doesn't magically fix poor image quality — it just removes the color information. So if possible, always start with the best-quality photo you have.

Third, some colors translate beautifully to grayscale (like skin tones, skies, and foliage with strong contrast) while others can look flat and muddy (like similarly-lit reds and greens, which can appear as almost identical grays). A good tool will let you adjust luminance channels before converting.

Step-by-Step: How to Convert a Photo to Black & White Online

Here's the complete process using a free browser-based tool. I'll use a general workflow that applies to most good converters, then mention tool-specific notes where needed.

1

Choose Your Tool

Open your browser and go to one of these trusted free tools: Pixlr Express (pixlr.com/express), LunaPic (lunapic.com), Canva Photo Editor (canva.com), or IMGonline (imgonline.com.ua). For most users, Pixlr Express is the best balance of speed, quality, and simplicity. Canva is great if you also want to add text or use it in a design. LunaPic is ideal for no-frills quick conversions.

✅ Tip: All of these work on mobile browsers too
2

Upload Your Photo

Click the "Upload" or "Open Image" button, then select your photo from your device storage. Most tools accept JPG, PNG, WEBP, and sometimes BMP files. If you're on a phone, you can directly pick a photo from your camera roll. Supported file sizes are usually up to 25MB, which is more than enough for any phone photo. If your photo is very large and slow to upload, you might want to reduce the image size first before converting.

📌 Supported: JPG, PNG, WEBP, BMP in most tools
3

Apply the Grayscale / B&W Filter

In the tool's menu, look for labels like Filters → Grayscale, Adjustments → Desaturate, or simply a "Black & White" button. In Pixlr, it's under Adjustments → Desaturate. In LunaPic, go to Adjust → Greyscale. In Canva, click on the photo, go to Edit Photo, and scroll to find the B&W filter. In IMGonline, the conversion to grayscale is the main function on the homepage. Click it and the preview will update in real time.

👁️ Always check the live preview before downloading
4

Adjust Contrast and Brightness (Optional but Recommended)

This step is what separates a flat, gray-looking conversion from a professional-looking one. After applying grayscale, increase the contrast slightly (try +10 to +20) and adjust the brightness if the image looks too dark or too washed out. Some tools also offer a "Curves" or "Levels" adjustment — these give you the most control over how shadows, midtones, and highlights appear. If your tool has a "Clarity" or "Sharpness" slider, a small boost (around +10) can make the result look crisper.

🎚️ +15 contrast usually makes a big visual difference
5

Choose Output Format and Download

Click Save or Download. Most tools will ask you to choose a format: JPG if you want a smaller file size (great for web use, WhatsApp, email), or PNG if you need lossless quality (for printing or design work). If you're converting a photo for a formal document, passport form, or ID application, save as PNG for sharpest results. For WhatsApp or general sharing, JPG at 80–90% quality is perfect. If you want to understand this format choice better, check out JPG vs PNG: Which Format Should You Use?

💾 Use PNG for printing, JPG for sharing
6

Verify the Result

Open the downloaded file and check it on your screen. Zoom in to make sure there's no blurriness introduced by compression. Check that the tones look natural — no blown-out whites or completely blocked-up shadows. If it doesn't look right, go back and adjust the contrast or brightness before re-downloading. Most tools let you undo and redo adjustments without starting over. If the file seems larger than expected, you can always compress the image to your target size as a final step.

🔍 Zoom in and check quality before sharing

Common Mistakes & Why They Happen

A lot of people try this and end up with a flat, lifeless gray image that doesn't look like what they imagined. Here's what usually goes wrong:

❌ Mistake 1: Not Adjusting Contrast After Converting

When you simply desaturate a photo, you remove color but all the contrast information that was carried by color disappears too. Red and green of similar lightness become the same gray. The result looks muddy and flat.

✅ Fix: After grayscale conversion, bump up contrast by +15 to +25. This recovers punch and depth.

❌ Mistake 2: Using a Low-Resolution Original

People sometimes compress or resize their photo before converting it to B&W, then wonder why the result looks pixelated. The conversion itself doesn't affect resolution, but a small original means a small output.

✅ Fix: Always convert from the highest resolution version you have. Resize or compress after conversion if needed.

❌ Mistake 3: Saving as PNG When JPG Would Do

For casual sharing, saving as PNG results in a much larger file size than necessary. A PNG black and white image can be 3–4x the size of the same JPG at 85% quality, with no visible difference on screen.

✅ Fix: Use JPG (80–90% quality) for WhatsApp, email, and social media. Reserve PNG for print or design work.

❌ Mistake 4: Using a Tool That Adds a Watermark

Some "free" tools are only free for a preview. When you download, they add a visible watermark. This is common on sites that make revenue by upselling to a Pro plan.

✅ Fix: Stick to Pixlr, LunaPic, IMGonline, or Canva (for simple edits). All offer genuinely watermark-free downloads for basic conversions.

❌ Mistake 5: Ignoring the Shadows in Portrait Photos

In portrait photography, dark clothing or hair can merge with a dark background in grayscale, making the subject look cut off at the edges or like they're floating. This happens because the contrast difference that existed in color disappears in grayscale.

✅ Fix: After converting, use the Levels or Curves tool to lift the dark areas slightly (pull up the shadows). This separates the subject from the background.

❌ Mistake 6: Converting an Already-Compressed Photo

If you've already compressed your image to a small KB size (say for WhatsApp), converting that to black and white usually reveals existing compression artifacts — blocky squares, blurry edges — more clearly because gray tones have less visual noise to hide them.

✅ Fix: Convert first, compress last. Always do color-to-B&W conversion on the highest quality file, then compress the output.

Frequently Asked Questions

These are the real questions people ask about converting photos to black and white online:

Is it really free to convert a photo to black and white online? +
Yes, genuinely free — no credit card, no hidden charges, and no signup required for basic conversions. Tools like LunaPic, Pixlr Express, and IMGonline are completely free to use with no watermark on downloads. Some tools like Canva or Adobe Express have premium features, but the basic grayscale conversion is free on all of them. If a tool asks you to pay before downloading a simple grayscale image, just use a different one.
Can I convert a photo to black and white on my Android phone? +
Absolutely. All the tools mentioned in this guide work in mobile browsers — Chrome on Android works perfectly. Open the browser, go to pixlr.com/express or lunapic.com, upload your photo from your camera roll (or gallery), apply the grayscale filter, and download. No app download needed. You can also use the native Photos or Gallery app on most Android phones: tap Edit → Filters → and look for a B&W or Grayscale option. However, online tools give you more control over contrast and output quality than the built-in phone filters.
Does converting to black and white reduce the image file size? +
Yes, generally it does — especially when saving as JPG. Removing color information means the compression algorithm has less data to encode, which typically results in a smaller file. For example, a 2.8MB color portrait might become a 1.1MB grayscale JPG. However, if you save as PNG (lossless), the file size won't reduce as dramatically because PNG stores every pixel value precisely. If you need a specific KB size for a form or upload limit, convert to B&W first, then use a separate compression tool to hit your exact target size.
Will converting to black and white affect the image resolution? +
No. Converting color to grayscale online does not change the pixel dimensions or resolution of your image. A 3000×4000 pixel photo remains 3000×4000 pixels after conversion. The only thing that changes is the color information in each pixel — each RGB triplet becomes a single gray value. If your converted image appears smaller or blurrier, it's either because the tool compressed it during processing (try a different tool or save as PNG) or because the original photo was already low resolution.
Why does my black and white photo look flat or washed out? +
This is the most common problem with simple desaturation. When you strip color, you lose the contrast that color was carrying — particularly when two similarly-lit but differently-colored areas (like a red and green object of the same brightness) become the same gray tone. The fix is to adjust contrast (+15 to +25) and use curves or levels to bring back depth in the shadows and highlights. Some advanced tools also let you adjust the luminance of specific color channels before conversion — darkening the red channel or brightening the yellow channel, for example, can create a much more dramatic and professional result.
What's the difference between grayscale and black and white? +
Grayscale uses 256 shades of gray, from pure white (255) to pure black (0), with every shade in between. This gives photos a smooth, realistic appearance. True "black and white" (also called bitmap or 1-bit) only uses two values: pure black and pure white. This creates a high-contrast, high-graphic look — like a woodcut print or a newspaper photograph from the early 20th century. For photography and most practical purposes, you almost always want grayscale. True B&W bitmap is used in specific graphic design or printing contexts. Most online tools labeled "black and white" actually convert to grayscale.
Can I convert a black and white photo back to color? +
Technically, no — not back to the original colors. Once color information is removed, it cannot be recovered from the grayscale file alone. However, AI colorization tools like DeepAI's colorizer or MyHeritage's colorization feature can intelligently guess and add colors to a grayscale photo based on what objects typically look like. The results can be surprisingly convincing, especially for portraits and natural scenes. But it's an estimation, not a true reversal — the resulting colors may differ significantly from the original photo's actual colors.
